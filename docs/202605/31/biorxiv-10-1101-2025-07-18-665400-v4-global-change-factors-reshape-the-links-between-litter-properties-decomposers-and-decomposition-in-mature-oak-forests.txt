Abstract 64 

1.)  Increasing atmospheric CO 2 concentrations alongside more frequen t and sever e drought s65 

are key global change factors impacting litter decomposition and global carbon cycle s. Yet, 66 

we have a poor understanding of how these pertu rbations impact interactions between 67 

initial litter chemical properties and the abiotic and biotic properties of the decomposition 68 

environment , especially under field conditions .69 

2.)  We tested how drought and elevated atmospheric CO 2 concentrations modify litter 70 

decomposition via litter properties and decompos ition environment using two sep arate ,71 

long -term manipulative drought or elevated CO 2 field experiments in mature oak 72 

woodland s. Litterbags were deployed in a reciprocal transplant design within each 73 

experiment , where we measured litter mass loss, carbon -bio chemistry , C:N ratios , moisture 74 

content, and microbial and mesofaunal properties .75 

3.)  We found that li tter placed in droughted plots decomposed slower than in control plots and 76 

experimental litter derived from elevated CO 2 plots decomposed slower over the first three 77 

harvests compared to control litter . Under drought, litter mass loss rates and C:N ratio was 78 

regulated by initial litter properties and the d ecomposition environment , while elevated 79 

CO 2 impacted mass loss via changes in initial litter properties. 80 

4.)  Synthesis : We show that drought and elevated atmospheric CO 2 can modify the 81 

decomposability of litter prior to litterfall and during the subsequent decomposition ,82 

highlight ing the need to disentangle their individual and interactive effects to better predict 83 

how global change factors influence decomposition. 84

Introduction 88 

Forest ecosystems cover 31% of global land cover (FAO, 2020) and play a critical role in 89 

global carbon (C) cycling (Harris et al., 2021)  as global forests take up 1.1 ± 0.8 Pg C year -1 (Pan 90 

et al., 2011) . Yet these ecosystems are being reshaped by global change factors , including 91 

increasing atmospheric CO 2 concentrations and increa sed frequen cy and intens ity of extreme 92 

weather events such as droughts (IPCC, 2021) . Elevated CO 2 and drought independently affect C93 

cycling because forests exhibit cellular - to ecosystem -level responses to both factors  (Arain et al., 94 

2022; Blaschke et al., 2001; Cech et al., 2003; Lee et al., 2021; Martínez -Sancho et al., 2022; 95 

Norby et al., 2024; Reay et al., 2025) . For example, in a mature stand of Eucalyptus tereticornis ,96 

elevated CO 2 increase d C uptake (through gross primary production) by 12% , al though net gains 97 

were lost due to an increase in soil fluxes, including from respiration  (Jiang et al., 2020) . In 98 

addition, a summer drought in 2022 resulted in nearly half of Europe’s soil moisture levels 99 

becoming critically low  (Toreti et al., 2022) , which corresponded with a reduced C uptake of ~60 100 

Tg C (van der Woude et al., 2023) . Because drought and elevated atmospheric CO 2 concentrations 101 

affect ecosystem C exchange, it is critical to understand how these global change factors influence 102 

key ecosystem processes connect ed to C cycling , such as litter decomposition. 103 

104 

Litter decomposition is driven by soil biota , which break down litter into simplified 105 

compounds , lead ing to plant uptake of nutrients (Krishna & Mohan, 2017) , the release of by -106 

products into the atmosphere , and incorporation of nutrients into various soil pools (Lavallee et al., 107 

2020) . The decomposer community consist of soil animals , including mesofauna , such as mites 108 

and collemb ol ans, and larger macrofauna like isopods, which impact decomposition through 109 

fragmentation of litter and grazing of microbes (Coleman et al., 2018; Seastedt, 1984) , in addition 110

120 

Drought and elevated atmospheric CO 2 can affect litter decomposition by modifying the 121 

litter and soil environment and decomposer community. Reductions in moisture content from 122 

drought s have cascading effects on soil processes and microbial and animal communities. 123 

Desiccation has differential effects on particular groups of organisms: for example, fungi are 124 

generally more resistant to drought than bacteria  (de Vries et al., 2012, 2018; Manzoni et al., 2012; 125 

Yuste et al., 2011) . Martin et al. (2024)  found that reduced precipitation decreased soil fauna 126 

abundance by 35%, while the effects of elevated CO 2 on decomposer communities are more subtle 127 

and often variable (Blankinship et al., 2011) . Although not nearly as intense as drought, elevated 128 

CO 2 concentrations can also potentially influence the soil microclimate stemming from changes 129 

to the canopy under enriched CO 2 concentrations (Franzaring et al., 2010) . In addition, a further 130 

distinction between elevated CO 2 and drought on the decomposition environment is from changes 131 

in C allocation below -ground. Elevated CO 2 and drought have contrasting effects on the flux of 132 

recent plant photosynthate, which in forests is a major pathway of energy flow to soils (Högberg 133

Both drought and elevated CO 2 concentrations affect leaf properties , such a s the C and 142 

nitrogen (N) content of leaves and shoots (Sun et al., 2020; Yuan & Chen, 2015) . Changes due to 143 

drought can stem from increased sugar accumulation, decreasing N content, and changes to 144 

primary and secondary plant metabolites (Chen et al., 2015; Orians et al., 2019; Wang & Wang, 145 

2025) . For elevated CO 2, N dilution within leaves together with C can alter the C :N ratio (Taub & 146 

Wang, 2008; Welti et al., 2020) . These and other changes to  litter properties  can affect litter quality 147 

and subsequently decomposer community composition (Gergócs & Hufnagel, 2016; Ilieva -148 

Makulec et al., 2006) . Changes to the decomposer community will subsequently impact the litter 149 

decomposition process but understanding how changes to both initial litter properties and 150 

decomposition environment impact animals and microbes remains poorly resolved .151

Prieto et al., (2019)  found strong interactive effects between litter origin and decomposition 155
