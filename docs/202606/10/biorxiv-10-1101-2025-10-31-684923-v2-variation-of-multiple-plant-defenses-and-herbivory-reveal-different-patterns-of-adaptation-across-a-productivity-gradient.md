## 摘要
具有广泛地理分布的植物在其分布范围内经历不同的生物和非生物条件，这些条件可能影响种群间的植物防御性状。与来自较冷、较干燥和较低生产力地区的种群相比，栖息在较温暖、较湿润和较高生产力地区的种群是否面临更大的压力并因此投资更多防御仍不确定。在这里，我们在大范围气候和生产力梯度上量化了卡罗来纳茄（Solanum carolinense L.）的防御性状和植食性。我们采集了覆盖卡罗来纳茄整个南北范围（北纬29-44度）的种群（33个种群；每个种群约15株植物），并进行了一项共同花园实验（15个种群；每个种群约5株植物）。我们检验了气候是否预测：1）植物防御性状（比叶面积、毛状体密度和糖苷生物碱浓度），2）植食者损害，以及3）田间和共同花园植物中性状与植食者损害的相关性。卡罗来纳茄在其分布范围中心的毛状体密度和植食者损害较高，而糖苷生物碱浓度在田间和共同花园中均与较温暖、较湿润的气候呈负相关。在田间，糖苷生物碱产量较高的植物受到的植食性较低，而在共同花园中，比叶面积较大的植物受到的损害更多。总体而言，这些发现表明同一物种内不同类型的防御性状可能遵循不同的生态和进化趋势，并强调在种内水平应用植物防御假说时需要针对性状进行具体考虑。

## Abstract
Plants with large geographic distributions experience varying biotic and abiotic conditions across their range that can influence plant defense traits among populations. Whether populations inhabiting warmer, wetter and more productive regions face greater pressure and results in higher investment in defense compared to populations from cooler, drier, and less productive regions remains uncertain. Here we quantified defense traits and herbivory on Solanum carolinense L. across a large climate and productivity gradient. We sampled populations spanning the entire north-south range (29-44{degrees} N) of S. carolinense, (33 populations; ~15 plants per population) and employed a common garden experiment (15 populations; ~5 plants per population). We examined whether climate predicts 1) plant defense traits (specific leaf area, trichome density and glycoalkaloid concentration), 2) herbivore damage, and 3) the correlation of traits and herbivore damage in field and common garden plants. Trichome density and herbivore damage were higher for S. carolinense at the center of its range, while glycoalkaloid concentrations were negatively associated with warmer, wetter climates both in the field and in the common garden. In the field, plants with higher glycoalkaloid production experienced reduced herbivory, while in the common garden plants with greater SLA received more damage. Overall, these findings suggest that different types of defensive traits within a single species may follow different ecological and evolutionary trends and highlight the need for trait-specific considerations when applying plant defense hypotheses at the intraspecific level.
