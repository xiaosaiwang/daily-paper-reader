ABSTRACT 18 

Understanding the genetic basis of ecological adaptation is a fundamental challenge of evolutionary 19 

biology . Spirochaetes are an ancient bacterial phylum of exceptional ecological breadth, 20 

encompassing major human pathogens such as the causative agents of syphilis, Lyme disease, and 21 

leptospirosis, alongside free -living species from contrasting environments and recently described 22 

spe cies that lack the canonical spiral morphology of the phylum . Here, using a curated dataset of 23 

genomes representing all cultivable spirochete species, we show that functional genome architecture 24 

(defined as the proportional allocation of coding capacity ac ross biological processes ) correlates with 25 

ecological lifestyle and phenotypic traits in Spirochaetes independently of phylogenetic relationships ,26 

with h ost -dependent lineages from distinct evolutionary origins converg ing on shared functional 27 

profiles . Phylum -wide p hylogenomic analyses identif ied Brachyspira as the earliest -diverging lineage, 28 

revis iting the evolutionary rooting of the phylum, and establish ing that the spiral morphology is 29 

ancestral and was lost in a single evolutionary transition also as sociated with coordinated functional 30 

changes. Lastly, a ncestral genome reconstruction uncovers the common ancestor as a motile, 31 

heterotrophic, and spiral -shaped bacteri um , with metabolic and structural features not previously 32 

described. Together, these res ults provide an integrated and functional framework for Spirochaetes ,33 

illustrating how genome architecture can be used to track the ecological diversification of a34 

widespread bacterial phylum. 35

INTRODUCTION 38 

Spirochaetes (or Spirochaetota ) form a coherent and ancient phylogenetic phylum  (1)  of exceptional 39 

ecological breadth, whose diversity of lifestyles offers a n opportunity  for understanding how closely 40 

related orga nisms adapt to radically different environments . The phylum  Spirochaetes  comprises a 41 

single class,  Spirochaetia , and includes four orders:  Brachyspirales , Brevinematales , Leptospirales ,42 

and  Spirochaetales . The order  Spirochaetales  is the largest, containin g five families and numerous 43 

genera, such as  Borrelia , Treponema , or  Spirochaeta  (2) . Spirochaetes  are widely distributed across 44 

diverse environments, occupying a broad range of ecological niches from free -living environmental 45 

species to host -associated lineages. Despite their common evolutionary origin, t hey inhabit highly 46 

contrasting environments, including highly alkaline lakes  (3) , the deep subsurface  (4) , or the gut 47 

microbiome  (5,6) . Members of this phylum display substantial metabolic diversity, existing as 48 

anaerobes, facultative anaerobes, microaerophiles, or aerobes . Several genera are of major medical 49 

and ecological importance, encompassing both pathogenic and non -pathogenic representatives. 50 

Other genera include symbiotic  spirochetes  inhabiting t he hindguts of termites and wood -eating 51 

cockroaches  (7) . Among the  human  pathogens,  Borrelia  spp. include the agents of Lyme disease ( B. 52 

bu rgdorferi ), one of the most prevalent vector -borne infections in North America and Europe, affecting 53 

several hundred thousand people annually  (8 –10) . Treponema  spp. include  T. pallidum , the causative 54 

agent of  over  50  million cases of syphilis worldwide  annually  (11) . Leptospirosis is a re -emergent 55 

zoonotic disease caused by pathogenic  Leptospira  species and accounts for approximately 1 million 56 

severe cases and 60,000 deaths every year  (12) . Lastly, novel taxa , some  uncultivated , are being 57 

described, but their significance remains to be elucidated  (1 3) .58

We next asked whether genome -wide functional information could capture ecological diversification 208 

that phylogenetic and genomic similarity metrics alone do not resolve . To characterize th e functio nal 209 

repertoire encoded across Spirochaetes , we annotated ortholog groups ( OGs ) using a cluster -based 210 

approach with fine -grained orthology (see Methods) . This strategy leverages high -confidence OG 211 

inference to propagate functional information across related sequences, thereby improving annotation 212 

in divergent proteins. Functional coherence within OGs was confirmed by individually annotating each 213 

sequence of the 491 most prevalent OGs (63423 proteins , 14.5% of the total dataset ), of which 96.3 %214 

of the annotated sequences showed consistent COG categories with in the ir OG , thus validating our 215 

approach . This method allowed us to assign a COG category to 58% ( N=19893) of all OGs , with an 216 

average 70% annotation level per species (ranging 48 –87% ) (Fig S 9). In addition, we successfully 217 

assigned a maximum annotation level to 83% ( N=28389 ) of all OGs , with an average 88 % annotation 218 

level per species (ranging 64 –100%) (Fig S 9). 219 

Interestingly, Leptospira spp. had a significantly higher number of OGs of putative eukaryotic origin 220 

than other Spirochaetes , including proteins related to actin cytoskeleton dynamics, growth regulation, 221 

and transcriptional control (Fig S10 , Table S3 ). Most of the se OGs were classified among the 222 

functional categories of posttranslational modification (O, 14 .2 %), signal transduction mechanisms 223 

(T, 12.2%) , lipid metabolism (I, 8 .8 %) or unknown function (S, 19.6% ) (Table S3 ). About a third of the 224 

eukaryotic OGs (N=44, 29.7%) were assigned to the Metazoa kingdom , 20.2% ( N=30) were assigned 225 

to the Fungi kingdom, and 17.6% ( N=26) were assigned to the Streptophyta phylum (Table S3 ). Other 226 

represented taxonomic levels were the Aconoidasida class (3.4%, N=5) or the Opisthokonta clade 227 

(1.4%, N=2). Remarkably, s ome of the assigned functions of the se OGs are typically associated with 228 

eukaryotic cellular processes and are uncommon in bacterial genomes, suggesting the presence of 229 

eukaryotic -like domains or proteins that may have been acquired through horizontal gene transfer or 230 

evolved convergently (Table S3 ) (35,36) . Given the ability of Leptospira species to colonize diverse 231 

hosts and environmental reservoirs, the presence of such eukaryotic -like functions m ay reflect 232 

molecular strategies for interacting with unicellular eukaryotes , niche adaptation or modulating host 233 

processes (37) .234

DISCUSSION 349 

In this study, we showed that functional genome architecture provides a robust and generalizable lens 350 

for interpreting ecological diversification in bacteria , a finding demonstrated here across the full 351 

ecological breadth of the very diverse Spirochaetes phylum . We determined that the proportional 352 

allocation of coding capacities across functional biological processes helps predict ecological 353 

lifestyles and phe notypic diversificatio n in Spirochaetes independently of phylogenetic relationships. 354 

Despite syphilis, Lyme disease and leptospirosis being emerging or re -emerging diseases, little is 355 

known about the biology of spirochetes compared to model bacteria. Spirochete research in both 356 

pathogenic and non -pathogenic species has traditionally been hampered by fastidious growth, limited 357 

genetic tools and incomplete genome annotations, complicating the progress of hypothesis -driven 358 

studies following omics analyses (42,43) . To address this, we significantly improved the functional 359 

annotation of spirochaetal proteins by applying a cluster -based approach, achieving an average 70% 360 

annotation level pe r species and nearly 90% annotation for some genomes. This represents a361 

significant improvement (∼15%) over previous studies, w here, for example, only 44% of the L. 362 

interrogans genome was annotated, compared to 6 4% in our analysis (24,44) . In addition, we 363 

assigned putative origin (bacterial, eukaryotic, viral or archaeal) and closest ortholog for 60% 364 

(N=8496) of genes of unknown function (S) , reducing the proportion of orphan genes in Spirochaetes 365 

to just 16% (N=5735) , a significant advancement for the field. 366

grained orthology. This strategy assumes that all sequences within an ortholog group share a 543

annotation pipelines. However, this approach requires ortholog groups inf erred with high precision 547

ortholog groups are therefore well -suited for this purpose, given their stringent evolutionary -aware 549 

inference and low false -positive r ates. Combined with the fine -grained ortholog annotations derived 550 

from eggNOG and eggNOG -mapper, this strategy improves annotation quality and coverage 551 

compared to other sequence -similarity -only pipelines. 552
